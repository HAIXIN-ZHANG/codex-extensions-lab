# Compact validation guide

Read only when choosing research or experiments, not for every quick product question. This is an optional decision aid, not a fixed questionnaire or numeric scoring system. The [PM Skills source](https://github.com/phuryn/pm-skills) was reviewed for assumptions and interviewing practices; no source text is bundled here.

For an early idea, check which uncertainty could invalidate the proposed product:

- **Need:** recent concrete behavior, frequency, consequences and existing workaround; avoid treating praise as demand.
- **Experience:** whether a person can complete the important task; a polished mockup alone does not answer this.
- **Business:** who decides/pays, what resources they already commit, delivery/support burden and a plausible acquisition path.
- **Delivery:** required data, integration/access and quality constraints; detailed feasibility belongs in technical design.

Use a short evidence table when useful: hypothesis; observed evidence/source/date; uncertainty; cheapest discriminating test; proposed criterion; next decision. Rank by consequence if wrong, weakness of evidence and effort to learn. Do not calculate precise scores from invented inputs. Low risk does not itself authorize implementation.

For interview preparation, ask about a specific recent occurrence, what the person actually did or spent, alternatives considered and constraints. Avoid pitching the idea first or treating hypothetical willingness as a purchase. Separate participant-reported facts from the researcher's interpretation. Drafting interview questions is not authorization to recruit or record people.

Use a small test that answers a decision, with results and thresholds clearly distinguished from proposals. Record negative evidence as well as positive signals; change or stop the idea when warranted. A report should make it possible to understand why the next design stage is justified, and what is still a hypothesis.
