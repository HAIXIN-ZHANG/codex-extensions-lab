# Grammar learning spine

This is a suggested sequence, not a diagnosis or an exam syllabus. Adapt pacing to an adult with substantial English exposure. Resume the saved plan before using this fallback. A diagnostic is optional; never invent a level or test result.

| Unit | Focus | Useful distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grammar-01 | Sentence structure, questions and negatives | Word order; be versus do |
| grammar-02 | Present simple and continuous | Habits, current actions, temporary states |
| grammar-03 | Past simple and continuous | Completed events and background actions |
| grammar-04 | Present perfect and past simple | Experience/relevance versus finished past time |
| grammar-05 | Perfect and perfect continuous | Result versus duration; past perfect as needed |
| grammar-06 | Future forms | Will, going to and arranged plans |
| grammar-07 | Modals and polite requests | Ability, obligation, advice and uncertainty |
| grammar-08 | Nouns, articles and quantifiers | Countability, a/an/the, some/any |
| grammar-09 | Prepositions and common patterns | Time/place, verb and adjective combinations |
| grammar-10 | Conditionals and wishes | Real versus hypothetical situations |
| grammar-11 | Relative and other clauses | Linking ideas without overlong sentences |
| grammar-12 | Passive and reported speech | Focus, reporting and time shifts |
| grammar-13 | Verb patterns and comparison | Infinitives/gerunds, adjectives/adverbs |
| grammar-14 | Consolidation from actual difficulties | Revisit demonstrated errors in new contexts |

Split large units into short chapters. Show meaning and use, contrast common alternatives, give bilingual examples and avoid rigid school rules where actual usage varies. Universal grammar can be taught with Australian situations. Do not describe standard shared English as uniquely Australian. A next chapter is a proposal until the learner chooses or asks to continue.
